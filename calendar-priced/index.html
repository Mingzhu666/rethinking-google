The problem

A calendar invite is free to send and expensive to receive.

Sending an invite costs the organizer$0
→
Attending it costs everyone else8 people × 1 hourplus the focus time it breaks in half

Economists call this an externality: the person making the decision doesn't pay for it. So we overproduce meetings, the same way we'd overproduce anything that's free to make.

"Won't people just hide the price?"

The price is shown to the organizer by default and only crosses a threshold into a required reason. It's a nudge, not a tax — Time Insights already shows time; this adds what that time is worth.

The business case

Meeting load is the top complaint in every engagement survey. A Workspace admin who can show "we gave back 2,000 focus hours this quarter" has a reason to stay on Workspace — and to buy Gemini for it.

Privacy by design

Costs use an admin-set org average, never individual salaries. Value checks are anonymous, unlock only after three answers, and show organizers a distribution — never a name.

How we'd prove it

A/B test at the org level: focus hours per person, meetings cancelled after value checks, and meeting satisfaction — not the number of invites sent.
