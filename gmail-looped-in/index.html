Google already blocks scam texts, flags spoofed calls, and lets family help you recover an account. Every one of them assumes you can judge the danger yourself.

Children can't read their parents' email every day, and parents don't want to be watched. The most damaging scams are slow relationships where no single message looks wrong. And many victims hesitated before paying — one nudge at that moment can change the outcome.

02

Three layers. A pause. A heads-up.

Rules clear most mail in microseconds; link reputation clears more; only what's left reaches Gemini, which reads intent — urgency, impersonation, a request for money or codes. Medium risks stay with Mom. High risks also reach Emma — as a risk, never as the email.

03

The scam is the conversation, not the message.

Romance and investment scams build trust for weeks before they ask. Scored one by one, every message looks friendly. Scored as a trajectory — contact → trust → isolation → the pitch → the ask — the danger is obvious.

Risks we designed for

Dignity and autonomy

Mom turns it on, picks who, sees every alert, and can turn it off. It never feels like surveillance because it isn't.

The abuser may be family

Guardians never see content, can't change settings, and can't stop Mom from turning it off.

False sense of safety

Positioned as one more nudge, not a guarantee. The pause is a question, never a block.

Scammers adapt

Gemini reads intent and trajectory, not keywords, so rewording the script doesn't get through.
